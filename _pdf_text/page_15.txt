3) LTC (LONG-TAIL COVERAGE)
While APLT captures the per-user fraction of tail items,
LTC measures system-level coverage, i.e., how broadly tail
items appear across all users’ top-N lists. As shown in
Figure 6, methods that aggressively promote tail content,
such as MUL and AUG, as well as exposure-correction
and representation/causality-oriented approaches like CausE,
DICE, and MACR, tend to achieve higher LTC by spreading
long-tail exposure across a larger portion of the cata-
log. A similar effect is observed for calibration-driven
approaches such as PPAC, which can also yield strong
tail coverage in some datasets by amplifying user-tail
alignment.
However, maximizing LTC via broad tail promotion
may come with the risk of overexposing niche items
for popularity-oriented users, potentially weakening per-
sonalization and calibration. In contrast, SUPER achieves
a consistently competitive (moderate-to-high) LTC across
datasets by controlling tail exposure at the user level rather
than enforcing a global objective. This user-centric exposure
control preserves meaningful long-tail coverage without
relying on blanket tail amplification. Methods such as PDRO
and CausalEPP, which prioritize robustness or exposure-
confounding correction, typically yield more conservative tail
coverage, reflecting their different optimization focus.
