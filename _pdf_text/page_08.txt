By adhering to the user’s estimated popularity inclination
ratio, SUPER tailors the final recommendation to reflect
each person’s established preferences while introducing
an appropriate degree of diversity. This calibration-based
mechanism simultaneously combats the tendency to overem-
phasize prevalent items (thereby mitigating popularity bias)
and ensures that the user’s legitimate tastes inform the
ultimate combination of head and tail items. In so doing, the
user is more likely to encounter content that resonates with
their observed tastes, encouraging an enriched and balanced
discovery experience across the broader catalog.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
In this section, we present the experimental setup designed to
assess the effectiveness of our Smart User-centric Popularity
Exposure Reduction for Fair and Diverse Recommendations.
We structure this evaluation by detailing the datasets
employed, the chosen evaluation metrics, the experimentation
methodology, and the baseline methods against which we
compare our proposed solution. The aim is to provide a
comprehensive and fair assessment of the approach’s ability
to mitigate popularity bias while remaining closely aligned
with individual user preferences. In forming our experimental
design, we draw inspiration from prior works that have
tackled popularity bias and related challenges [10], [20], [27].
A. DATASETS
To ensure the generalizability of our findings, we rely on
three widely studied benchmark datasets: MovieLens-1M
(ML),1 Douban Book (DB), 2 and Yelp.3 These datasets are
commonly used in recommender system research and differ
substantially in domain, size, and sparsity, thereby allowing
us to examine how varying data characteristics influence the
performance and robustness of our approach.
• ML: Focuses on movie preferences, featuring over one
million user ratings on a five-star scale. Its moderately
dense structure offers a stable testbed for evaluating the
debiasing capabilities of our method.
• DB: Extracted from a large-scale online book com-
munity, this dataset includes user-item interactions
also recorded on a five-star scale. Its higher sparsity
and larger catalog size enable analysis under more
challenging conditions.
• Yelp:Captures user reviews on local businesses, ranging
from restaurants to service providers. The high sparsity
and niche-oriented nature of these interactions make the
Yelp dataset particularly suited to testing how well our
approach handles long-tail and diverse user interests.
All rating values across these datasets are discrete and span
the [1–5] interval. The diversity in size, average rating
patterns, and sparsity ratios mirrors real-world scenarios,
ensuring that our conclusions are not narrowly tied to a single
domain [9], [21].
B. EVALUATION METRICS
We evaluate the proposed method using a compact yet
comprehensive set of metrics that capture recommenda-
tion accuracy, popularity calibration, and beyond-accuracy
objectives such as diversity and novelty. Following prior
work on popularity bias and user-centric evaluation [8],
[27], we deliberately omit Precision, Recall, and F1-score,
as these metrics do not directly reflect popularity exposure or
calibration effects.
