2) DATASET SPARSITY AND DOMAIN CHARACTERISTICS
Dataset characteristics further influence this behavior.
In denser datasets such as MovieLens-1M, user popularity
inclination can be estimated more reliably, resulting in
particularly strong calibration and stable ranking behavior.
In contrast, highly sparse datasets such as Yelp pose greater
challenges due to limited interaction histories. Despite this,
SUPER remains robust and competitive, indicating that it
does not rely on overly precise popularity estimates and can
function effectively even under severe sparsity.
3) USER ACTIVITY LEVELS AND COLD-START ROBUSTNESS
To further examine robustness with respect to user activity,
we stratified users into three groups according to their
total number of interactions: low-activity (<20), medium-
activity (20–100), and high-activity (>100). For each group,
we re-evaluated all debiasing methods across all datasets and
baseline recommenders.
The results for low-activity (cold-start) users indicate
that Yelp is largely dominated by sparse profiles (13,685
out of 16,239 users; 84.27% have < 20 interactions),
where calibration is inherently challenging due to limited
behavioral evidence. In this regime, RMSE-PC values remain
small in absolute magnitude, yet clear relative differences
still emerge across debiasing strategies. Notably, SUPER
achieves the lowest average RMSE-PC of 0.0096 across
backbone recommenders, outperforming strong baselines
such as PDRO (0.0234), PPAC (0.0294), DICE (0.0435),
and CausalEPP (0.0254). Even compared to competitive
calibration-oriented methods like Fair (0.0249) and CausE
(0.0377), SUPER maintains the best calibration, providing
quantitative evidence of robustness under sparse interaction
histories. For medium- and high-activity users, where the
user-specific popularity inclination Popu can be estimated
more reliably, SUPER remains among the top-performing
methods in calibration while preserving competitive accuracy
and beyond-accuracy performance.
a: USER-CENTERED FAIRNESS VS. SYSTEM-LEVEL EXPOSURE
While metrics such as Entropy, LTC, and Gini-type mea-
sures capture how evenly recommendation exposure is
distributed across items at the system level, they do not
directly reflect fairness at the individual user level. In this
work, user-centered fairness is primarily evaluated through
calibration-oriented metrics (Rmse-PC and MRMC), which
quantify how well recommendation exposure aligns with
each user’s historical popularity inclination.
Taken together with the above segment analyses, the results
suggest that SUPER adapts smoothly across diverse user
profiles without introducing systematic disadvantages to any
particular group. Rather than enforcing uniform exposure
across users, SUPER prioritizes aligning recommendation
exposure with individual user tendencies, thereby providing
a user-centric notion of fairness that remains robust under
heterogeneity in both user activity and dataset sparsity.
I. DISCUSSION OF OVERALL PERFORMANCE
Taken collectively, the experimental results across calibra-
tion, beyond-accuracy, and ranking accuracy dimensions
demonstrate that SUPER not only aligns recommended
content with users’ popularity preferences, but also consis-
tently enhances diversity, long-tail exposure, and novel item
discovery. While several debiasing techniques outperform
SUPER in isolated metrics, such as MUL in terms of
APLT, xQuad with respect to Entropy, or causality- and
representation-driven methods like CausE and DICE in
catalog-level exposure, these gains are frequently accompa-
nied by increased calibration error or instability across ranked
positions.
Methods that rely on strong global constraints or uni-
form corrective mechanisms often overshoot the intended
proportion of tail items, leading to misalignment with indi-
vidual users’ historical popularity inclinations. In particular,
approaches such as CausE, DICE, and MACR may effectively
mitigate exposure bias at an aggregate level, yet struggle
to preserve rank-level coherence or user-specific balance,
especially in sparse or heterogeneous datasets. Conversely,
purely accuracy-driven baselines tend to maintain relevance
but offer limited diversity, novelty, and long-tail coverage.
By coupling user-specific popularity inclination with a
dynamically balanced item selection strategy, SUPER adapts
robustly to both moderate and sparse data conditions. This
adaptability benefits not only calibration-oriented measures
(Rmse-PC, MRMC), but also promotes equitable item
exposure and meaningful exploration without resorting to
indiscriminate tail promotion. As a result, SUPER achieves
a more favorable overall trade-off across accuracy, fairness,
and diversity objectives. More broadly, these findings suggest
that popularity debiasing should not be framed solely as
a diversity-enhancement or exposure-correction task, but
rather as a structured calibration problem that requires bal-
ancing relevance, exposure, and user-specific consumption
patterns. The consistent performance of SUPER indicates
that regulating the positional structure of exposure, rather
than relying exclusively on global optimization objectives,
can produce systemic improvements across multiple recom-
mendation dimensions. This perspective highlights exposure
structure as a controllable design variable in recommender
systems, rather than a byproduct of ranking optimization,
and underscores the importance of rank-aware calibration for
achieving user-aligned recommendation quality.
