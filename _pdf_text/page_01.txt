To address these issues, a wide range of popularity-bias

While effective at the aggregate level, many of these methods

of misalignment between recommendations and user tastes.
Recent research therefore highlights the importance of
modeling user-specific popularity tendencies, aiming to
reduce preference–exposure confounding factors such as

A. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND MOTIVATION
Despite notable progress, existing popularity-bias mitigation
approaches often fail to capture the nuanced and evolving
nature of users’ preferences. A common implicit assumption
is that interactions with popular items directly reflect a
user’s affinity for mainstream content. In practice, however,
users may engage with trending or prominently displayed
items due to convenience, discounts, or social exposure
rather than genuine interest. Treating such interactions as
stable preference signals can mislead RSs and produce
recommendation lists that increasingly diverge from users’
stable preference patterns (as observed in interactions).
We note, however, that without impression logs it is gen-
erally not possible to identify causal user preferences from
observational interaction data alone. Therefore, in this work,
popularity inclination is modeled as a behavioral proxy used
for calibration rather than as a ground-truth latent construct.
For instance, consider a user with only a small number
of interactions. Even within limited histories, users often
exhibit observable differences in their consumption exposure
patterns, some concentrate on widely consumed items,
while others disproportionately engage with niche content.
Although such signals do not constitute causal preference,
they provide a practical behavioral basis for estimating
a user-specific popularity-inclination proxy and calibrating
head-tail exposure under standard offline recommendation
settings.
Many existing methods rely on post-processing re-ranking
to elevate tail items [10], [17], [20] or introduce fairness
and diversity constraints to rebalance exposure [21], [22].
Although effective at improving catalog-level fairness, these
techniques often operate on static or aggregate representa-
tions of user behavior. More recent work has moved toward
user-aware modeling by considering popularity proportions
within user profiles [26], [27] or incorporating contextual and
temporal factors [7], [23], [24], [25]. Nevertheless, accurately
separating underlying preference signals from incidental
exposure remains a key challenge.
These observations motivate the need for a user-centric
popularity-debiasing framework that explicitly models each
user’s inclination toward popular versus tail content and uses
this inclination to calibrate the final Top-N recommendation
list. Such an approach aims to mitigate popularity bias with-
out sacrificing personalization, enabling balanced, diverse,
and preference-aligned recommendations.
Accordingly, the goal is not to replace existing debi-
asing paradigms, but to provide a structurally simple and
model-agnostic mechanism that stabilizes popularity expo-
sure at the user level. This perspective positions popularity
calibration as a complementary control layer rather than a
competing learning objective.
B. RESEARCH QUESTIONS AND CONTRIBUTIONS
This study introduces a smart user-centric popularity-
debiasing framework that integrates user-specific popularity
inclination into both the training and post-processing stages
of recommendation generation. The framework is guided by
the following research questions:
RQ1: How can we estimate a robust behav-
ioral proxy of users’ popularity inclination
under interaction-only logs, while mitigating
exposure–preference conflation?
RQ2: How can we integrate user-specific pop-
ularity tendencies into a hybrid recommendation
framework that combines pre-processing strategies
(training on popular and tail subsets separately)
with a post-processing calibration step to ensure
balanced and personalized top-N lists?
RQ3: How can we rigorously evaluate this smart
method’s effectiveness in mitigating popularity bias
while maintaining or improving overall recommen-
dation quality, diversity, and user satisfaction?
To address these questions, we make the following
contributions:
1) User-Centric Popularity Profiling: We propose a
refined technique that goes beyond simplistic measures
of popularity engagement, employing the Pareto prin-
ciple to identify and characterize a user’s observed
leaning toward popular or niche content.
2) Hybrid Pre- and Post-Processing Framework:
Leveraging these user profiles, we introduce a hybrid
approach where popular and tail item subsets are inde-
pendently trained, then dynamically blended according
to each user’s detected popularity inclination.
3) Comprehensive Evaluation Paradigm: We develop
a robust evaluation framework incorporating con-
ventional accuracy metrics as well as measures of
diversity, fairness, and user satisfaction. This provides
a holistic understanding of the system’s ability to
mitigate popularity bias while aligning with individual
preferences.
