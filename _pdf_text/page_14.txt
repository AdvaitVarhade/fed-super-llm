2) ENTROPY
Entropy measures how evenly recommendations are dis-
tributed across the item catalog, reflecting overall diversity.
As illustrated in Figure 5, approaches such as xQuad, MUL,
and MACR frequently attain high entropy values by explicitly
diversifying recommendation lists or enforcing balanced
exposure across item groups. However, such gains are
often accompanied by weaker alignment with user-specific
popularity tendencies, particularly when diversification is
applied uniformly.
In addition, user-aware optimization and calibration
frameworks such as PDRO and PPAC, as well as
exposure-correction strategies like CausalEPP, tend to
exhibit more moderate entropy behavior: they mitigate pop-
ularity effects through robustness, calibration, or exposure
penalization objectives rather than aggressively maximizing
catalog dispersion. As a result, their entropy gains are
typically less pronounced than strongly diversification-driven
re-ranking methods, but may be achieved with improved
rank-level coherence.
