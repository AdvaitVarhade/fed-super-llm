2) POPULARITY-DEBIASING BENCHMARKS
We compare SUPER against established post-processing
popularity-debiasing methods that operate independently of
the backbone recommender:
• FA*IR [35]: A fairness-aware re-ranking method that
enforces balanced exposure between head and tail items.
• Discrepancy Minimization (DM) [34]: An
exposure-balancing approach that aligns item recom-
mendation frequencies with a target distribution.
• xQuad [20]: A diversification-based re-ranking strategy
that balances relevance and popularity categories.
• Aug and Mul [47]: Weight-based re-ranking methods
that penalize popular items using additive or multiplica-
tive adjustments.
• VaR[4], [32]: A value-aware ranking approach favoring
long-tail items through inverse popularity weighting.
• CausE [53]: A causal embedding method that disentan-
gles preference from exposure bias.
• DICE [54]: A disentanglement-based model separating
user interest from conformity effects.
• MACR[55]: A counterfactual post-processing approach
correcting popularity-induced distortions.
• PDRO [36]: A distributionally robust optimization
framework designed to stabilize recommendation qual-
ity under popularity-skewed interaction distributions.
• PPAC [37]: A personalized calibration approach that
dynamically adjusts recommendation exposure accord-
ing to user-specific popularity sensitivity.
• CausalEPP [38]: A causal debiasing method that
penalizes exposure-driven interactions to better separate
preference from popularity effects.
All baseline methods are evaluated under a unified
experimental protocol with consistent parameter tuning
based on their original publications. This ensures a fair
and transparent comparison across different modeling and
debiasing paradigms.
V. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
This section presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation
of the proposed SUPER framework in the context of
popularity bias mitigation. Our experimental design reflects
the current state of the literature by incorporating a broad
spectrum of debiasing strategies, ranging from classical
re-ranking and exposure-balancing methods to recent user-
aware, optimization-based, and causality-driven approaches.
Specifically, we compare SUPER against a diverse
set of competing strategies covering multiple method-
ological paradigms, including re-ranking-based approaches
(AUG, MUL, VaR, DM, Fair, xQuad), causality- and
representation-driven methods (CausE, DICE, MACR), and
recent user-aware optimization frameworks such as PDRO,
PPAC, and CausalEPP.
All debiasing methods are evaluated on top of five
widely adopted backbone recommenders, HPF, NeuMF,
SKMeans, VaeCF, and WBPR, across three benchmark
datasets: ML (MovieLens-1M), DoubanBooks, and Yelp. The
diversity of both recommenders and datasets enables a robust
examination of performance under varying sparsity levels,
interaction densities, and domain characteristics.
All experiments are repeated with five random seeds
to reduce sensitivity to stochastic effects, and we report
seed-averaged mean performance for all methods. To assess
whether SUPER’s improvements are consistently observed
across random initializations, we conduct paired Wilcoxon
signed-rank tests over matched seeds. Each test therefore
evaluates consistency rather than large-sample statistical
power, yielding five paired observations per comparison.
For completeness, Wilcoxon tests are computed for all
debiasing method pairs within each dataset–recommender–
metric setting. To maintain clarity and avoid visual overload,
we report only comparisons between SUPER and the
second-best debiasing method, defined deterministically as
the strongest non-SUPER baseline according to seed-averaged
mean performance for the corresponding metric (higher-is-
better for nDCG, APLT, Entropy, LTC, Novelty, and GKPI;
lower-is-better for Rmse-PC and MRMC). This reporting
strategy provides a conservative and focused assessment by
benchmarking against the strongest competing alternative
rather than weaker baselines. Statistical significance is
annotated directly in the figures using star notation, where
(*), (**), and (***) denote p < 0.05, p < 0.01, and
p < 0.001, respectively, and is shown only when SUPER
achieves the best debiasing performance for that setting.
Collectively, this experimental design prioritizes breadth
of comparison, statistical robustness, and methodological
fairness, thereby providing a reliable foundation for assessing
whether user-centric, rank-aware popularity calibration can
deliver consistent gains across diverse recommendation
environments. Importantly, this design allows us to evaluate
whether calibration-oriented exposure control can remain
effective even when compared against strong, recently
proposed debiasing frameworks.
Since popularity alignment is widely recognized as an
important factor in recommendation quality, we begin
by analyzing calibration performance before turning to
beyond-accuracy and ranking metrics.
