A global view of these metrics, presented in Figure 2 for
Rmse-PC and Figure 3 for MRMC, indicates that SUPER
achieves consistently lower calibration errors than a broad
spectrum of popularity-debiasing strategies. Our experimen-
tal benchmark covers (i) re-ranking-based debiasing methods
(AUG, MUL, VaR), (ii) representation- or causality-driven
approaches (CausE, DICE, MACR), and (iii) user-aware
optimization frameworks (PDRO, PPAC,CausalEPP).
Although PDRO and PPAC explicitly model robustness or
personalized calibration objectives, their empirical behavior
reveals notable calibration instability. Across all datasets and
backbone recommenders, these methods frequently exhibit
substantially higher Rmse-PC values, indicating difficulties
in aligning recommendation popularity with users’ historical
tendencies. The gap becomes even more pronounced under
the rank-sensitive MRMC metric, where both methods
often produce markedly elevated miscalibration scores. This
pattern suggests that optimizing for distributional robustness
or adaptive weighting alone may not sufficiently regulate
where exposure occurs within the ranked list.
