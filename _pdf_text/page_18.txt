F. POPULARITY-THRESHOLD SENSITIVITY
We analyzed the sensitivity of SUPER to the head–tail
popularity threshold by evaluating multiple head thresholds
(10%, 20%, 30%, 40%, and 50%) across all datasets and
backbone recommenders. The results reveal clear trends
across metrics. Lower thresholds may slightly improve
calibration (RMSE-PC and MRMC) by adopting a stricter
notion of popularity, whereas larger thresholds generally
weaken calibration and reduce the overall balance among
objectives. When jointly considering accuracy, calibration,
and beyond-accuracy metrics through GKPI, the 20%
threshold consistently provides the most robust trade-off,
achieving the highest average GKPI across datasets and
serving as the best-performing choice in the majority of
configurations. While a small number of dataset–backbone
combinations favor alternative thresholds, these deviations
are limited and do not undermine the overall stability of the
20% setting. Accordingly, we adopt 20% as a stable default
for comparability, while noting that the threshold can be
selected in a fully data-driven manner by maximizing GKPI
on a validation split.
G. COMPONENT-WISE ABLATION OF SUPER
To better understand the contribution of each component
in SUPER, we conducted an ablation study with two
variants. The full SUPER model combines Pareto-based
head–tail partitioning, a user-specific popularity-inclination
ratio Popu, and the blueprint-based merge. In Variant A,
we removed personalization by replacing Popu with a fixed
50/50 head–tail split for all users, while keeping both the
Pareto partition and the blueprint unchanged. In Variant B,
we retained the Pareto partition and the user-specific Popu,
but replaced the blueprint with a random merge that only
preserves the overall head–tail ratio in expectation.
Across all datasets and baseline recommenders, Variant A
consistently exhibits higher Rmse-PC than SUPER, with
the average calibration error increasing from approximately
0.035 to 0.065, and a slight decrease in the average GKPI,
indicating that the user-specific Popu is the main driver of
SUPER’s calibration gains. In contrast, Variant B yields
almost identical Rmse-PC to SUPER but slightly lower GKPI
on average, suggesting that the blueprint-based merge mainly
contributes to improving the holistic trade-off captured
by GKPI rather than to calibration alone. Since all three
configurations share the same Pareto-based partitioning, its
effect is primarily reflected in the improvements of SUPER
(and its variants) over the non-debiased baselines reported in
the main experiments. These trends were consistent across all
datasets and backbone recommenders; for brevity, we do not
report the full ablation table.
Overall, these ablation results empirically validate the
design of SUPER by showing that both user-specific popu-
larity inclination and blueprint-based merging are necessary
to achieve strong calibration and a balanced holistic perfor-
mance.
