• Root Mean Square Error in Popularity Calibration
(Rmse-PC) : [44] This metric measures how closely
the popularity level of the recommended top-N items
aligns with each user’s interaction-derived popularity-
inclination proxy. Unlike interaction-based measures,
Rmse-PC relies only on enjoyed items (items rated above
the user’s mean rating), thereby reducing exposure-
driven noise. Lower values indicate better popularity
calibration.
• Mean Rank Miscalibration (MRMC): MRMC evalu-
ates how well the cumulative popularity distribution of
a ranked recommendation list matches the user’s histor-
ical head–tail consumption pattern. It computes rank-
wise divergence between historical and recommended
popularity distributions and averages this miscalibration
across users. Lower MRMC values correspond to better
alignment.
• nDCG (Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain):
We report nDCG to assess ranking quality in terms
of relevance and position sensitivity [21], [33]. Higher
values indicate better accuracy.
• Average Percentage of Long-Tail Items (APLT):
APLT measures the proportion of tail items in the top-N
recommendation lists, reflecting the system’s tendency
to promote niche content [45]. Higher values indicate
stronger long-tail exposure.
• Entropy: Entropy quantifies the dispersion of recom-
mended items across the catalog, based on the frequency
with which items appear in users’ top-N lists [8]. Higher
entropy indicates more balanced item exposure.
• Long-Tail Coverage (LTC):LTC measures the fraction
of tail items that appear at least once in the union of
all recommendation lists [45], capturing global catalog-
level coverage.

• General Key Performance Indicator (GKPI): A
Comprehensive Performance Measure
Recommender system performance is inherently multi-
dimensional, as accuracy-oriented and beyond-accuracy
metrics often reflect competing objectives. To facilitate a
concise comparison across methods, we report the Gen-
eral Key Performance Indicator (GKPI) as a compact
summary measure that aggregates multiple evaluation
dimensions into a single score.
GKPI combines a core accuracy metric (nDCG) with
four beyond-accuracy metrics (APLT, Novelty, Entropy,
and LTC). Rather than replacing these metrics, GKPI
serves as an auxiliary indicator that highlights whether a
method achieves a balanced trade-off between relevance
and popularity-aware objectives.
To avoid dominance by any single dimension, each
beyond-accuracy metric is paired with nDCG using a
harmonic-type mean:
H(x, y) = 2xy
x + y. (2)
The harmonic mean penalizes extreme imbalance,
ensuring that strong performance in one dimension can-
not fully compensate for weak performance in another.
This property is particularly suitable for popularity
debiasing, where over-optimizing diversity or novelty
may significantly harm relevance.
GKPI is then computed as the arithmetic mean
of the four harmonic combinations between nDCG
and each beyond-accuracy metric. Equal weighting is
intentionally adopted to avoid introducing additional
hyperparameters or domain-specific assumptions, and to
maintain neutrality across evaluation dimensions in the
absence of a universally accepted prioritization scheme.
